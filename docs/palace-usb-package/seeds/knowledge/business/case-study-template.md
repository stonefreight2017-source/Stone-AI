## Customer Story Structure

### The Core Framework: Problem → Solution → Result (PSR)

Every case study tells the same fundamental story: someone had a problem, they tried your product, and they got results. The art is in the specifics.

### Full Case Study Structure

```
1. Quick Stats Bar (top of page — scannable)
2. Customer Overview (who they are)
3. The Challenge (what problem they faced)
4. Why They Chose [Product] (evaluation process)
5. The Solution (how they implemented)
6. The Results (measurable outcomes)
7. Customer Quote (emotional highlight)
8. What's Next (future plans)
9. CTA (reader's next step)
```

## Section-by-Section Template

### 1. Quick Stats Bar

Place this at the very top. Executives and busy readers will scan this and decide whether to read further.

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  INDUSTRY: SaaS          COMPANY SIZE: 1-10         PLAN: Plus │
│  TIME TO VALUE: 1 week   AGENTS USED: 12    ROI: 340% in 90d  │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Rules**:
- 4-6 data points maximum
- Include industry (helps readers self-select relevance)
- Include at least one ROI or outcome metric
- Use the customer's tier/plan (social proof for pricing)

### 2. Customer Overview

**Length**: 50-100 words

**Template**:
```
{Company Name} is a {industry} company based in {location}. Founded in {year} by {founder_name}, the company {brief description of what they do}. With a team of {size}, they serve {customer base} and {key business metric — e.g., "process over 10,000 orders monthly" or "manage $2M in annual recurring revenue"}.
```

**Example**:
```
DataFlow is a B2B SaaS company based in Austin, Texas. Founded in 2024 by Sarah Chen, the company builds data pipeline tools for mid-market companies. With a team of 4, they serve over 200 customers and manage $380K in annual recurring revenue.
```

**Rules**:
- Include company size (helps readers assess relevance)
- Include a business metric (establishes scale)
- Keep it factual — no superlatives or subjective claims
- Get permission for all details shared

### 3. The Challenge

**Length**: 150-250 words

This is where you earn the reader's attention. The challenge must feel REAL and RELATABLE.

**Template**:
```
### The Challenge

Before Stone AI, {company} faced {primary challenge}.

"{Direct quote from customer about the problem}" — {Name, Title}

Specifically, the team was dealing with:

• **{Pain point 1}**: {Specific detail — hours lost, money spent, opportunity missed}
• **{Pain point 2}**: {Specific detail}
• **{Pain point 3}**: {Specific detail}

{Optional: What they tried before and why it didn't work}

The impact was clear: {quantified cost of the problem — time, money, missed opportunities, team frustration}.
```

**Example**:
```
### The Challenge

Before Stone AI, DataFlow's 4-person team was stretched impossibly thin. As a technical founder, Sarah spent 15+ hours per week on non-technical tasks: writing blog posts, analyzing customer data, drafting email campaigns, and managing support tickets.

"I was the CEO, the content team, the data analyst, and the support agent — all at once. The quality of everything suffered because nothing got my full attention." — Sarah Chen, Founder & CEO

Specifically, the team was dealing with:

• **Content bottleneck**: Publishing 1 blog post per month instead of the 4 needed for SEO traction
• **Data blind spots**: Customer analytics were done "when there's time" — which was never
• **Tool sprawl**: 7 different AI and productivity tools, none of which talked to each other
• **Context loss**: Every ChatGPT session started from scratch, re-explaining the business every time

The impact was clear: DataFlow was leaving an estimated $50K/year in growth on the table due to inconsistent content, slow customer insights, and operational drag.
```

### 4. Why They Chose Stone AI

**Length**: 100-150 words

**Template**:
```
### Why Stone AI

{Customer} evaluated {number} alternatives before choosing Stone AI. The deciding factors were:

1. **{Factor 1}**: "{Brief quote about this factor}"
2. **{Factor 2}**: {One-sentence explanation}
3. **{Factor 3}**: {One-sentence explanation}

"{Quote about the decision moment}" — {Name, Title}
```

**Example**:
```
### Why Stone AI

Sarah evaluated 5 alternatives — including dedicated AI writing tools, virtual assistant services, and hiring a part-time operations person. The deciding factors for Stone AI were:

1. **Specialized agents**: "I didn't need one AI that's OK at everything. I needed specialists — a real copywriter, a real analyst, a real strategist."
2. **Persistent memory**: Agents that remembered DataFlow's product, customers, and brand voice between sessions
3. **Cost**: $49.99/month for 30 agents vs. $3,000+/month for freelancers or a part-time hire

"The math was obvious. But what sealed it was the memory. I explained my business once, and the agents just... knew it from then on." — Sarah Chen
```

```
**{Workflow 2 name}**:
{Agent used} → {What it does} → {Output}
{Frequency}

**{Workflow 3 name}**:
{Agent used} → {What it does} → {Output}
{Frequency}

**Setup time**: {How long from sign-up to productive use}

**Key configuration decisions**:
• {Decision 1 and why}
• {Decision 2 and why}

"{Quote about the implementation experience}" — {Name, Title}
```

### 6. The Results

**Length**: 200-300 words

This is the money section. Make it unmistakably clear and data-driven.

**Template**:
```
### The Results

After {timeframe} with Stone AI, {company}'s results:

| Metric | Before | After | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| {Metric 1} | {value} | {value} | {percentage or absolute change} |
| {Metric 2} | {value} | {value} | {percentage or absolute change} |
| {Metric 3} | {value} | {value} | {percentage or absolute change} |
| {Metric 4} | {value} | {value} | {percentage or absolute change} |

**Headline result**: {The single most impressive outcome in one sentence}

**Secondary results**:
• {Result with specific data}
• {Result with specific data}
• {Result with specific data}

**Unexpected benefit**: {Something positive they didn't anticipate}

"{Quote about the results — emotional or data-driven}" — {Name, Title}
```

**Example**:
```
### The Results

After 90 days with Stone AI, DataFlow's numbers told the story:

| Metric | Before | After | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Content output | 1 post/month | 12 posts/month | +1,100% |
| Ops time (Sarah) | 15 hrs/week | 4 hrs/week | -73% |
| Tool cost | $180/month | $49.99/month | -72% |
| Customer insight reports | 0/month | 4/month | N/A (new capability) |

**Headline result**: Sarah reclaimed 11 hours per week — the equivalent of hiring a part-time operations person.

**Secondary results**:
• Blog traffic increased 340% over 90 days due to consistent content publishing
• Customer churn decreased 18% after implementing data-driven retention insights
• Email open rates improved from 19% to 31% using the Copywriter agent's subject line testing

**Unexpected benefit**: The agents' collaboration feature allowed Sarah to chain outputs — the Data Analyst would identify trends, then the Copywriter would turn those into customer emails. This workflow didn't exist before.

"I didn't just save time. I started doing things I never had capacity for. The data analysis alone has probably saved us $20K in churn." — Sarah Chen
```

### 7. Customer Quote (Highlight)

**Length**: 1-3 sentences

Pull the single best quote from the interview for a visual highlight/callout.

```
> "Stone AI didn't just replace my tools — it gave me capabilities I never had as a solo founder. I went from surviving to actually strategizing."
> — Sarah Chen, Founder & CEO, DataFlow
```

**Rules for selecting the highlight quote**:
- Emotional > analytical (save the data for the results section)
- Transformation-focused (before/after implied)
- Specific to your product (not generic "great product" praise)
- Short enough to read in one breath

## Data Points to Collect

### Interview Questions for Case Study Subjects

**Background**:
1. What does your company do, and who do you serve?
2. How large is your team?
3. What were you using before Stone AI?

**Challenge**:
4. What was your biggest operational challenge before Stone AI?
5. How much time/money were you spending on the tasks Stone AI now handles?
6. What did you try before Stone AI? Why didn't it work?
7. What was the cost of NOT solving this problem?

**Solution**:
8. Which agents do you use most? For what tasks?
9. How long did it take to get productive with Stone AI?
10. Did you customize any workflows? What does your setup look like?

**Results**:
11. What specific metrics improved? (time saved, cost reduced, output increased)
12. What was the single biggest impact?
13. Was there anything unexpected — a benefit you didn't anticipate?
14. How has Stone AI changed how you work day-to-day?

**Quote Capture**:
15. If you had to describe Stone AI to a friend in one sentence, what would you say?
16. What would you say to someone considering switching from their current tools?

### Metric Collection Checklist

- [ ] Time saved (hours/week or hours/month)
- [ ] Cost reduction (specific dollar amount or percentage)
- [ ] Output increase (content pieces, reports, tasks completed)
- [ ] Revenue impact (direct or attributed)
- [ ] Tool consolidation (number of tools replaced)
- [ ] Efficiency gain (before/after for specific workflows)
- [ ] Quality improvement (if measurable — error rates, satisfaction scores)
- [ ] Time to value (sign-up to first productive use)

## Distribution Strategy

### Where to Publish Case Studies

1. **Dedicated case study page** on stone-ai.net/customers
2. **Blog post** version (more narrative, SEO-friendly)
3. **PDF download** (gated for lead capture, or ungated for maximum reach)
4. **Landing page social proof** (excerpted stats and quotes)
5. **Email campaigns** (relevant case study sent to matching segments)
6. **Sales follow-ups** (send relevant case study after demos)
7. **Social media** (LinkedIn post, X thread, Reddit post)
8. **Pricing page** (relevant quotes near tier descriptions)

### Repurposing a Case Study

One case study becomes 8+ content pieces:

```
Full Case Study
├── LinkedIn post (highlight + link)
├── X thread (5-7 tweets telling the story)
├── Blog post (narrative version)
├── Email snippet (for upgrade nudge sequences)
├── Landing page quote block
├── Pricing page testimonial
├── Sales deck slide
└── Ad creative (stat + quote)
```

## Case Study Quality Standards

### Before Publishing Checklist

- [ ] Customer has reviewed and approved all content
- [ ] All data points are accurate and verified with the customer
- [ ] Quotes are exact (not paraphrased without permission)
- [ ] Company name and individual names are spelled correctly
- [ ] No confidential information is disclosed
- [ ] Results include timeframe ("in 90 days" not just "saved 73%")
- [ ] At least 3 quantified metrics in the results section
- [ ] Clear CTA at the end
- [ ] Formatted for web (headers, bullet points, stat blocks)
- [ ] Mobile-responsive layout tested
- [ ] SEO elements in place (title tag, meta description, alt text)

### Case Study Collection Cadence

**Target**: 1 new case study per month

**Selection criteria** (prioritize customers who):
1. Represent a key ICP segment
2. Have quantifiable results
3. Are willing to be named publicly
4. Use a paid tier (demonstrates value)
5. Represent a different industry or use case than existing studies

### Maintaining a Case Study Library

Organize by:
- **Industry**: SaaS, E-commerce, Agency, Service Business
- **Company size**: Solo, 2-10, 11-50, 50+
- **Plan tier**: Starter, Plus, Smart, Pro
- **Primary benefit**: Time saved, cost reduced, output increased, new capability
- **Primary agent**: Copywriter, Data Analyst, Developer, Strategy

This makes it easy to pull the right case study for the right prospect in sales conversations.
