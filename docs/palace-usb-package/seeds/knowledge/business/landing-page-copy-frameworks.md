## The Three Core Frameworks

### 1. PAS — Problem, Agitation, Solution

The most reliable conversion framework for SaaS. Works because humans are wired to avoid pain before seeking pleasure.

#### Structure

**Problem**: Name the exact pain your reader is experiencing. Be specific — not "managing tasks is hard" but "you're spending 3 hours a day copying data between tools that should already talk to each other."

**Agitation**: Twist the knife. Show the reader the full cost of their problem — time lost, revenue missed, team frustration, competitive disadvantage. Make them FEEL it.

**Solution**: Present your product as the natural resolution. The relief. The bridge from their painful present to their desired future.

#### PAS Applied to Stone AI

**Problem**: "You've tried AI assistants. They give you generic answers, forget your context, and can't actually DO anything in your workflow."

**Agitation**: "Every time you start a new chat, you're back to square one. Explaining your business. Repeating your preferences. Watching an AI confidently give you advice that doesn't apply to your situation. Meanwhile, your competitors are shipping faster, making better decisions, and spending less time on the busywork you're still drowning in."

**Solution**: "Stone AI gives you 44 specialized agents that remember your context, work together as a team, and actually execute — not just advise. From code to copy to strategy, your AI workforce is ready."

#### PAS Variations

**Mini-PAS** (for sections within a page):
- One sentence per element
- Works for feature descriptions, FAQ intros, testimonial setups

**Extended PAS** (for long-form landing pages):
- Problem section: 2-3 paragraphs with specific scenarios
- Agitation: Data points, competitor comparisons, time calculations
- Solution: Feature walkthrough tied directly to each pain point

**Inverted PAS** (for audiences aware of their problem):
- Start with agitation (they already know the problem)
- Briefly name the problem to confirm alignment
- Spend more time on solution details

### 2. AIDA — Attention, Interest, Desire, Action

The classic marketing framework. Best for structured landing pages where you need to walk visitors through a logical progression.

#### Structure

**Attention**: A headline or visual that stops the scroll. Pattern interrupt. Something unexpected, bold, or deeply specific.

**Interest**: Build on the attention with relevant details. Features, data points, unique mechanisms. Answer "tell me more."

**Desire**: Transform interest into want. Social proof, success stories, before/after, exclusivity, scarcity. Answer "I want this."

**Action**: Clear, single CTA. Remove friction. Address the last objection. Make it easy.

#### AIDA Applied to Stone AI

**Attention**: "What if your entire team was AI — and actually competent?"

**Interest**: "Stone AI deploys 44 specialized agents across copywriting, development, security, strategy, data analysis, and more. Each agent has deep domain expertise. They work together, share context, and execute tasks — not just chat about them."

**Desire**: "Companies using Stone AI ship 3x faster, cut operational costs by 60%, and make data-driven decisions in minutes instead of days. Join 2,000+ founders who replaced their tool stack with one intelligent workforce."

**Action**: "Start free. No credit card. Your first 4 agents are waiting. [Get Started →]"

#### AIDA Section Mapping for Landing Pages

| AIDA Stage | Page Section | Typical Length |
|---|---|---|
| Attention | Hero headline + subhead | 10-20 words |
| Interest | Feature grid / How it works | 200-400 words |
| Desire | Social proof / Case studies | 150-300 words |
| Action | CTA block | 20-50 words |

#### AIDA Timing Rules

- Attention must hit in < 3 seconds (above the fold)
- Interest must sustain through 1 scroll
- Desire must peak before the CTA appears
- Action must be visible without hunting

### 3. BAB — Before, After, Bridge

The simplest framework. Paints two pictures and your product is the bridge between them. Best for emotional products or lifestyle transformations.

#### Structure

**Before**: The reader's current reality. Paint it vividly. Use their language.

**After**: The reader's desired reality. What life looks like with their problem solved.

**Bridge**: Your product is how they get from Before to After.

#### BAB Applied to Stone AI

**Before**: "Right now, you're juggling 12 different tools, context-switching every 15 minutes, and spending more time managing your workflow than actually doing the work. Your AI assistant is a glorified search engine that forgets everything between sessions."

**After**: "Imagine opening one platform and having a team of specialists ready to go. Your copywriter drafts the email sequence while your data analyst pulls the cohort report while your developer ships the API endpoint. They all know your business, your preferences, your standards."

**Bridge**: "Stone AI is that bridge. 44 specialized agents. One platform. Zero context loss. Start free and meet your team."

#### When to Use BAB vs PAS vs AIDA

| Scenario | Best Framework |
|---|---|
| Audience knows they have a problem | PAS |
| Audience needs education first | AIDA |
| Audience is emotionally driven | BAB |
| Technical B2B audience | AIDA or PAS |
| Consumer/lifestyle product | BAB |
| High-price-point product | AIDA (more logical progression) |
| Quick conversion (ads, emails) | PAS (compact) |

## Headline Formulas

### The 12 Headline Archetypes

#### 1. The Specific Number
"44 AI Agents That Actually Execute — Not Just Chat"
Formula: `[Number] [Things] That [Desirable Outcome]`

#### 2. The How-To
"How to Ship 3x Faster Without Hiring a Single Employee"
Formula: `How to [Desired Outcome] Without [Undesired Cost]`

#### 3. The Question
"What Would You Build If You Had a Full Team — For Free?"
Formula: `What Would You [Desire] If [Barrier Removed]?`

#### 4. The Direct Command
"Stop Switching Between 12 Tools. Start Building."
Formula: `Stop [Pain Activity]. Start [Desired Activity].`

#### 5. The Testimonial
"'Stone AI Replaced My Entire Operations Stack' — Founder, TechCo"
Formula: `"[Quote About Transformation]" — [Credible Source]`

#### 6. The Curiosity Gap
"The AI Platform That Makes Developers Nervous (In the Best Way)"
Formula: `The [Product] That [Unexpected Reaction] ([Positive Qualifier])`

#### 7. The Comparison
"ChatGPT Gives You Answers. Stone AI Gives You a Team."
Formula: `[Competitor/Alternative] [Gives/Does X]. [You] [Give/Do Y].`

#### 8. The Elimination
"Never Explain Your Business to an AI Again"
Formula: `Never [Pain Point] Again`

#### 9. The Timeframe
"From Zero to Full AI Workforce in 5 Minutes"
Formula: `From [Starting Point] to [Desired State] in [Short Time]`

#### 10. The Social Proof
"2,000+ Founders Replaced Their Tool Stack With Stone AI"
Formula: `[Impressive Number] [People Like Reader] [Did Desirable Thing]`

#### 11. The Specificity
"44 Agents. 6 Languages. 1 Platform. $0 to Start."
Formula: `[Specific Detail]. [Specific Detail]. [Specific Detail]. [Call to Value].`

#### 12. The Contrarian
"You Don't Need More AI Tools. You Need Fewer."
Formula: `You Don't Need [What They Think]. You Need [What You Offer].`

### Headline Testing Hierarchy

1. **Test the promise first** — Which benefit resonates most?
2. **Test the format second** — Question vs statement vs command?
3. **Test specific words third** — "team" vs "workforce" vs "agents"?
4. **Test length last** — Short punchy vs detailed specific?

### Subheadline Rules

- Subheadline EXPLAINS the headline, doesn't repeat it
- If headline is emotional → subheadline is logical
- If headline is specific → subheadline provides context
- Maximum 25 words for subheadline
- Include at least one concrete detail (number, timeframe, mechanism)

## Hero Section Copy Patterns

### Pattern 1: Headline + Subheadline + CTA
The most common. Clean and direct.

```
H1: Your AI Workforce, Ready to Deploy
Sub: 44 specialized agents that remember your context, work together, and actually execute.
CTA: [Start Free] [Watch Demo]
```

### Pattern 2: Headline + Bullet Points + CTA
Best for multiple value props.

```
H1: The AI Platform Built for Founders Who Ship
• 44 specialized agents across every business function
• Persistent memory — never re-explain your business
• Agents collaborate autonomously on complex tasks
CTA: [Get Started — Free] [See Agents →]
```

### Pattern 3: Headline + Social Proof + CTA
Best when you have strong numbers.

```
H1: Stop Managing AI. Start Managing Your Business.
Social: Trusted by 2,000+ founders · 4.9/5 rating · $2M+ in customer revenue influenced
CTA: [Join Free]
```

### Pattern 4: Headline + Mini-Story + CTA
Best for emotional connection.

```
H1: We Built the Team We Couldn't Afford to Hire
Body: When we started Stone AI, we needed a copywriter, a developer, a data analyst, and a strategist — but our budget said "pick one." So we built all of them. Now you can have them too.
CTA: [Meet Your Team]
```

### Hero Section Rules

1. **One CTA maximum in the hero** (secondary link is OK, not a second button)
2. **Hero must answer three questions in < 5 seconds**: What is this? Who is it for? What do I do next?
3. **No jargon in the hero** — save technical details for below the fold
4. **Visual must reinforce the copy** — not distract from it
5. **Mobile hero**: Headline must make sense in 2 lines max on a 375px screen

## Social Proof Placement Strategy

### The Social Proof Hierarchy (Strongest to Weakest)

1. **Named customer with photo + specific result**: "Sarah Chen, founder of DataFlow — 'Stone AI cut our ops time by 73%'"
2. **Aggregate data with specificity**: "2,147 founders · 1.2M tasks completed · 99.7% uptime"
3. **Logo bar**: Known company logos (requires B2B customers at scale)
4. **Star ratings with count**: "4.9/5 from 847 reviews"
5. **Generic testimonial**: "'Great product!' — Happy Customer"
6. **Trust badges**: Security certifications, press mentions, awards

### Placement Rules

**Above the fold**: Only aggregate data or star ratings. Don't slow down the hero with long testimonials.

**After first value prop section**: Place 1-2 detailed testimonials. Reader just learned what you do → now prove it works.

**Before pricing**: Place the strongest social proof. Reader is evaluating cost → reduce risk perception.

**After pricing**: Place a testimonial about ROI or value. Reader is deciding → confirm the investment is worth it.

**Near every CTA**: Micro-social-proof within 200px of every call-to-action. Even a simple "Join 2,000+ founders" works.

### Social Proof Copy Formulas

**Result-focused**: "[Customer] [achieved specific result] in [timeframe] using [product]"

**Transformation-focused**: "Before Stone AI, [customer] [struggled with X]. Now [they/their company] [achieves Y]."

**Specificity-focused**: "[Customer] saved [$amount/hours/percentage] on [specific task] in the first [timeframe]."

**Emotion-focused**: "'I can't imagine going back to [old way]. [Product] changed how we [key activity].'" — [Name, Title]

## CTA Optimization

### CTA Copy Formulas

#### Action + Value
- "Start Free" (action: start, value: free)
- "Get Your AI Team" (action: get, value: AI team)
- "Build Faster Today" (action: build faster, value: today/immediacy)

#### Specificity CTAs
- "Deploy Your First Agent" (specific action)
- "See All 44 Agents" (curiosity + specificity)
- "Start With 4 Free Agents" (specific + value)

#### Risk-Removal CTAs
- "Try Free — No Credit Card"
- "Start Free, Upgrade When Ready"
- "See It In Action — 2 Min Demo"

### CTA Button Hierarchy

**Primary CTA**: High contrast, action-oriented, appears first
**Secondary CTA**: Lower contrast or text link, alternative path (demo, learn more)

**Never use**:
- "Submit" (no value communicated)
- "Click Here" (no context)
- "Sign Up" (sounds like work)
- "Buy Now" (too aggressive for SaaS above $20/mo)

### CTA Surrounding Copy

**Above the CTA**: Reinforce the value or remove an objection
**Below the CTA**: Micro-copy that reduces friction

Examples:
```
[Start Free →]
No credit card required · Setup in 2 minutes · Cancel anytime
```

```
Join 2,000+ founders already building with Stone AI
[Get Started — Free]
Free forever plan available
```

### CTA Placement Frequency

- **Minimum**: 3 CTAs on any landing page (hero, mid-page, footer)
- **Maximum**: 1 CTA per scroll-height (don't overwhelm)
- **Rule**: Every section that builds desire should end near a CTA
- **Mobile**: Sticky CTA bar after first scroll recommended

### Feature Section Copy Template

```
[Icon]
## [Feature Name — 3-5 words]
[One sentence: What it does + why it matters — max 20 words]

[Optional: 2-3 bullet points with specific details]
```

Example:
```
🧠
## Persistent Agent Memory
Your agents remember every conversation, preference, and decision — so you never repeat yourself.

• Context carries across sessions automatically
• Each agent builds expertise specific to YOUR business
• Zero ramp-up time, every time
```

### Pricing Page Copy Structure

1. **Headline**: "Simple Pricing. Powerful Agents." or "Pick Your Team Size."
2. **Tier cards**: Name, price, agent count, key differentiator, CTA
3. **Feature comparison table**: Full breakdown with checkmarks
4. **FAQ section**: Address pricing objections
5. **Final CTA**: "Start free and upgrade when you're ready"

### FAQ Copy Formulas

**Format**: Question should be what the reader is ACTUALLY thinking (conversational), not corporate.

Bad: "What are the terms of service for subscription management?"
Good: "Can I cancel anytime?"

Bad: "How does the agent allocation system function across tiers?"
Good: "What happens if I need more agents?"

**Answer structure**:
1. Direct answer (first sentence)
2. Brief explanation if needed
3. Reassurance or CTA

Example:
Q: "Is the free plan actually free?"
A: "Yes — forever. No credit card required, no trial period, no hidden limits. You get 4 agents with full functionality. Upgrade whenever you want, or don't. We're not going anywhere."

## Copy Testing and Iteration

### What to A/B Test on Landing Pages

**High Impact** (test first):
1. Headline (biggest single lever)
2. CTA copy and color
3. Hero section layout
4. Social proof type and placement

**Medium Impact** (test second):
5. Subheadline copy
6. Feature descriptions
7. Pricing page layout
8. FAQ content

**Low Impact** (test last):
9. Body copy word choices
10. Image selection
11. Footer content
12. Navigation copy

### Copy Measurement Framework

| Metric | What It Tells You | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Bounce rate | Headline/hero relevance | < 40% |
| Scroll depth | Content engagement | > 60% to CTA |
| Time on page | Copy interest level | 2-5 min (landing) |
| CTA click rate | Desire + clarity | > 3% |
| Conversion rate | Overall copy effectiveness | > 2% (SaaS) |
| Exit page | Where copy loses them | Identify and fix |

### The Copy Iteration Loop

1. **Baseline**: Launch with best-guess copy using frameworks above
2. **Observe**: 1 week of traffic minimum before conclusions
3. **Hypothesize**: "Changing [element] from [A] to [B] will improve [metric] because [reason]"
4. **Test**: Run A/B test with adequate sample size (see ab-testing-methodology.md)
5. **Analyze**: Statistical significance required — no gut calls
6. **Implement**: Winner becomes new baseline
7. **Repeat**: Next highest-impact element

## Voice and Tone Guidelines for Stone AI

### Brand Voice Attributes

1. **Confident, not arrogant**: "We built something powerful" not "We're the best AI ever"
2. **Direct, not blunt**: "Here's what it does" not "Figure it out"
3. **Technical, not jargon-heavy**: Explain mechanisms without requiring a CS degree
4. **Ambitious, not hypey**: "Build faster" not "REVOLUTIONARY GAME-CHANGER"
5. **Human, not corporate**: "We get it" not "We understand your operational challenges"

### Words We Use vs Words We Avoid

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| Agents | Bots |
| Execute | Leverage |
| Build | Synergize |
| Ship | Optimize (overused) |
| Team | Ecosystem |
| Fast | Lightning-fast |
| Works | Seamlessly integrates |
| Smart | AI-powered (redundant for us) |

### Tone by Context

| Context | Tone |
|---|---|
| Landing page hero | Bold, confident, clear |
| Feature descriptions | Informative, specific, benefit-led |
| Pricing page | Transparent, reassuring, value-focused |
| Error messages | Helpful, calm, actionable |
| Onboarding | Friendly, guiding, encouraging |
| Documentation | Clear, precise, scannable |
| Marketing emails | Personal, conversational, value-first |

## Page-Level Copy Checklists

### Pre-Launch Copy Checklist

- [ ] Headline passes the "5-second test" — would a stranger understand the value?
- [ ] Every section answers "so what?" from the reader's perspective
- [ ] CTA appears at least 3 times on the page
- [ ] Social proof appears near every CTA
- [ ] No section is longer than 2 scrolls without a CTA or visual break
- [ ] Mobile copy reads naturally (no awkward line breaks in headlines)
- [ ] All claims are specific and defensible (not "the best" without proof)
- [ ] FAQ addresses top 5 objections
- [ ] Page has one clear primary action (not competing CTAs)
- [ ] Copy is scannable — headers, bullets, bold text guide the eye

### Post-Launch Copy Checklist

- [ ] Heatmap shows visitors reaching the CTA sections
- [ ] Scroll depth > 60% average
- [ ] CTA click rate > 3%
- [ ] Bounce rate < 40%
- [ ] Top exit pages identified and copy revised
- [ ] A/B test running on highest-impact element
- [ ] Copy updated to reflect latest features/pricing
- [ ] Testimonials are current (< 6 months old)

## Advanced Techniques

### The "One Reader" Principle

Write to ONE person, not an audience. Before writing any section, define:
- **Who**: "Solo founder, technical, 28-35, building a SaaS"
- **Where**: "Evaluating AI tools, has tried ChatGPT and Copilot"
- **Feeling**: "Frustrated with context loss, excited about AI potential"
- **Wanting**: "A tool that actually works WITHOUT babysitting"

Every word should feel like you're talking to THIS person, not broadcasting.

### The Specificity Principle

Vague claims erode trust. Specific claims build it.

Bad: "Save time with AI"
Better: "Save 3 hours per day on operational tasks"
Best: "Our users save an average of 3.2 hours per day — that's 16 hours per week back in your calendar"

### The Objection Inoculation Technique

Address objections BEFORE the reader thinks of them.

"You might be thinking: 'Another AI platform? I've tried three this year.' Fair. Here's why Stone AI is different: our agents don't just answer questions — they execute. They remember. They collaborate. Try it free and decide for yourself."

### The Curiosity Loop

Open a loop in one section, close it in a later section. Keeps readers scrolling.

Hero: "40 agents that work like a real team. (Wait until you see how they collaborate.)"
Later section: "Here's how agent collaboration actually works: [explanation]"
