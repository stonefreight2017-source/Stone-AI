## 1. AIDA Framework (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action)

### The Framework
1. **Attention**: Grab them. Stop the scroll.
2. **Interest**: Hold them. Make them curious.
3. **Desire**: Make them want it. Connect to their needs.
4. **Action**: Tell them what to do. Clear CTA.

### Applied to Stone AI Landing Page

**Attention (Headline)**
"Stop Googling. Start Getting Expert Answers."

Why it works: Addresses a universal pain (Googling for answers), positions Stone AI as the alternative, uses a command structure that demands attention.

**Interest (Subheadline + First Section)**
"Stone AI gives you 44 specialist AI agents — each one trained for a specific job. Writing, coding, strategy, research, finance, marketing, and more. One subscription. Every expert you need."

Why it works: Specific number (44), specific capabilities, "one subscription" addresses the multi-tool fatigue.

**Desire (Value Demonstration)**
"Imagine asking a dedicated coding expert about your bug — not a general chatbot that kind of knows Python. Or getting business strategy from an AI trained in competitive analysis, not one that read a Wikipedia summary.

That's Stone AI. Real specialists. Real depth. Real answers."

Why it works: Contrast between generic AI and specialist AI. Paints a picture the reader can see themselves in.

**Action (CTA)**
"Start free. 4 agents. No credit card."
[Get Started →]

Why it works: Three short statements address the three main objections (cost, commitment, risk). The CTA is specific and action-oriented.

### AIDA for Upgrade Prompt (In-App)

**Attention**: "You just asked about financial modeling."
**Interest**: "Our Financial Analyst agent is specifically trained for this — projections, unit economics, scenario modeling."
**Desire**: "It's like having a finance MBA on call."
**Action**: "Upgrade to PLUS to unlock it. Just $49.99/month." [Upgrade →]

### AIDA Rules
- Attention must be instant. You have 2 seconds on a landing page, 1 second in an in-app prompt.
- Interest must be specific. Vague claims ("great AI") don't hold attention.
- Desire must be emotional AND rational. "Imagine..." (emotional) + specific capabilities (rational).
- Action must be crystal clear. One button, one instruction, no ambiguity.

## 2. PAS Framework (Problem, Agitate, Solution)

### The Framework
1. **Problem**: Name the user's pain.
2. **Agitate**: Make the pain feel urgent. Twist the knife.
3. **Solution**: Present your product as the relief.

### Applied to Upgrade Prompts

**Problem**
"You're on the STARTER plan with 16 agents. That means 26 specialists are locked — including the ones that handle [specific use case relevant to user's recent activity]."

**Agitate**
"Every time you need help with [that use case], you're either getting a general answer from your current agents or leaving Stone AI to find help elsewhere. That's time wasted and quality lost."

**Solution**
"PLUS unlocks 30 agents — including the specialists you've been needing. For $30 more per month, you get the right expert for every question."
[Upgrade to PLUS →]

### PAS for Landing Page Section: "Why pay when ChatGPT is free?"

**Problem**
"You've tried ChatGPT. You asked it to write your business plan, debug your code, and craft your marketing copy. It gave you... okay answers."

**Agitate**
"'Okay' doesn't cut it when your business is on the line. ChatGPT is a generalist — it knows a little about everything but doesn't specialize in anything. Your marketing copy sounds generic. Your code has subtle bugs. Your business plan reads like a template."

**Solution**
"Stone AI has 44 specialist agents. The Marketing Agent writes like a marketer. The Code Agent debugs like a developer. The Strategy Agent thinks like a consultant. Specialists beat generalists every time."
[Try 4 Free Agents →]

### PAS for Win-Back Email

**Problem**
"Since you cancelled Stone AI, you've probably gone back to juggling multiple tools — or settling for one-size-fits-all AI."

**Agitate**
"How many tabs do you have open right now? ChatGPT in one, Google in another, maybe a writing tool in a third? That's not efficient — that's exhausting."

**Solution**
"Come back to Stone AI and get every specialist in one place. We've improved since you left — [specific improvement]. Here's 30% off your first month back."
[Reactivate →]

### PAS Rules
- The Problem must be real and recognizable. If the reader doesn't identify with the problem, the rest fails.
- Agitate doesn't mean be negative — it means make the problem vivid. Help them feel it.
- The Solution must directly address the Problem. Don't solve a different problem than the one you named.

## 3. BAB Framework (Before, After, Bridge)

### The Framework
1. **Before**: The user's current state (with the problem).
2. **After**: The user's desired state (problem solved).
3. **Bridge**: How your product gets them from Before to After.

### Applied to Case Studies

**Before**
"Sarah runs a small e-commerce business. She was spending 3 hours a day writing product descriptions, social media posts, and customer emails. She tried ChatGPT but the output was too generic — she had to heavily edit everything."

**After**
"Now Sarah spends 30 minutes on the same tasks. Her product descriptions are polished, her social posts get engagement, and her customer emails sound personal. She has time for the work that actually grows her business."

**Bridge**
"Sarah upgraded to Stone AI PLUS. The Writing Agent handles her product descriptions. The Marketing Agent crafts her social posts. The Communication Agent drafts her emails. Three specialists, one subscription, 2.5 hours saved daily."

### BAB for Pricing Page

**Before**: "You're using a general AI chatbot. It's okay at everything but great at nothing."
**After**: "You have a team of 44 AI specialists. Each one excels at their domain."
**Bridge**: "Stone AI. Plans start at $0. Upgrade when you're ready."

### BAB for Testimonials Structure
Every testimonial should follow BAB:
- Before: "I was struggling with [problem]..."
- After: "Now I [benefit]..."
- Bridge: "Stone AI's [specific agent/feature] made the difference."

### BAB Rules
- Before must be relatable. The reader should think "that's me."
- After must be aspirational but believable. Don't promise miracles.
- Bridge must be specific. Name the product, the feature, the agent that caused the transformation.

## 4. Objection Handling

### Objection: "Why pay when ChatGPT is free?"

**Response Framework: Acknowledge, Reframe, Prove**
1. **Acknowledge**: "ChatGPT is great for casual questions."
2. **Reframe**: "But when you need expert-level help, you need a specialist, not a generalist."
3. **Prove**: "Our Code Agent catches bugs that ChatGPT misses. Our Strategy Agent uses frameworks like OODA and First Principles. Our Writing Agent matches your brand voice."

**Copy version**: "ChatGPT is a Swiss Army knife. Stone AI is a toolbox with 44 professional-grade tools. Both have their place — but when the job matters, you want the right tool."

### Objection: "I can't justify another subscription."

**Response Framework: Value Comparison**
1. "You're probably paying for [writing tool] ($X/mo), [coding tool] ($Y/mo), and [other tool] ($Z/mo)."
2. "Stone AI replaces all of them. PLUS is $49.99/month for 30 specialist agents."
3. "That's not another subscription — it's one subscription that replaces three."

**Copy version**: "Stop stacking subscriptions. One plan. Every specialist. Starting at $19.99/month."

### Objection: "Is AI really that useful for my work?"

**Response Framework: Low-Risk Trial**
1. "We get it — AI sounds like hype until you try it."
2. "That's why we offer 4 agents completely free. No credit card. No commitment."
3. "Try it on a real task. If it doesn't impress you, you've lost nothing."

**Copy version**: "Skeptical? Good. Start free. We'll let the results convince you."

### Objection: "I don't have time to learn another tool."

**Response Framework: Simplicity**
1. "Stone AI is a chat interface. If you can send a text message, you can use Stone AI."
2. "No training needed. No learning curve. Type your question, get an expert answer."
3. "Your first conversation takes 30 seconds."

**Copy version**: "If you can text, you can use Stone AI. Ask a question, get an expert answer. That's it."

### Objection: "What makes this different from other AI tools?"

**Response Framework: Differentiation Stack**
1. "44 specialist agents, not one generalist."
2. "A personal Bestie companion that remembers you."
3. "Tiers designed for every budget — including free forever."
4. "Built by a founder who uses it daily, not a corporation shipping features by committee."

**Copy version**: "Other AI tools give you one brain. We give you 44 specialists, a personal companion, and a community. No comparison."

### Objection: "The expensive tiers aren't worth it."

**Response Framework: Value Per Day**
1. "SMART is $3.33/day. That's less than a coffee."
2. "You get 39 specialist agents powered by Claude Sonnet — the premium AI model."
3. "If one agent saves you 30 minutes of work per day, that's 15 hours/month. What's your hourly rate?"

**Copy version**: "39 AI specialists for $3.33/day. If they save you 30 minutes daily, you're getting paid to use them."

## 5. Social Proof Patterns

### Types of Social Proof

**User Count**
- "Join X users who trust Stone AI."
- Only use this when the number is impressive. "Join 47 users" hurts more than it helps. Wait until you can say "Join 1,000+" or "Join 10,000+."
- Alternative for early stage: "Trusted by users in X countries" or "X conversations this month."

**Testimonials**
- Real quotes from real users. Include name, role/context, and photo if possible.
- Follow the BAB structure: problem → solution → result.
- Best testimonials are specific: "Stone AI's Code Agent found a bug in my Python script that I'd missed for 3 days" beats "Great product, love it!"

**Trust Badges**
- Security: "AES-256 encryption" "Your data is secure"
- Quality: "Powered by Claude Sonnet" (for SMART/PRO tiers)
- Commitment: "No credit card required for free tier" "Cancel anytime"

**Activity Proof**
- "X conversations happening right now" (if real-time data is available).
- "X users upgraded this week."
- "Most popular: PLUS plan."
- These create FOMO and social validation.

**Expert/Authority Proof**
- If any notable person or organization uses Stone AI, mention them.
- "Used by developers, writers, entrepreneurs, and consultants."
- Industry recognition, awards, media mentions.

### Social Proof Placement
- **Landing page**: Above the fold (user count), mid-page (testimonials), near CTA (trust badges).
- **Pricing page**: Near tier cards ("Most Popular" badge on target tier), below pricing table (testimonials from each tier).
- **Checkout**: Trust badges (secure payment, cancel anytime, money-back guarantee).
- **In-app upgrade prompts**: "X users upgraded to PLUS this month."

### Social Proof Rules
1. **Never fake it.** Fake testimonials, inflated numbers, and fabricated badges destroy trust permanently.
2. **Be specific.** "Great product" is weak. "Saved me 2 hours on my marketing report" is strong.
3. **Match proof to audience.** A developer testimonial on the Code Agent page. A writer testimonial on the Writing Agent page.
4. **Update regularly.** Stale social proof (testimonials from 2 years ago) signals an inactive product.

## 6. CTA Psychology

### Urgency
- Creates pressure to act now rather than later.
- Genuine urgency: "Promo ends Friday." Time-limited offers.
- False urgency: "Only 3 seats left!" for unlimited digital products. Never do this — users can tell.
- For Stone AI: "First month for $9.99 — this week only." Must be real.

### Scarcity
- "Limited spots" doesn't apply to SaaS (unlimited digital access).
- But scarcity of promotions works: "This discount won't be offered again."
- Time-limited free trials create scarcity: "Your 7-day STARTER trial ends in 3 days."

### Loss Aversion
- People are more motivated to avoid losing something than to gain something.
- "Don't lose access to your 39 agents" > "Get access to 39 agents."
- In cancellation flow: "You'll lose access to [specific things]" is more effective than "You could keep [specific things]."
- For upgrade prompts: "You're missing answers from 26 locked agents every time you ask a question."

### Specificity
- Vague CTAs ("Get Started") are weaker than specific ones ("Start Chatting with 4 Free Agents").
- The CTA should tell the user exactly what happens when they click.
- "Try the Code Agent free" > "Sign up" > "Get started" > "Learn more."

### CTA Button Copy Best Practices
- Use first person: "Start My Free Trial" > "Start Your Free Trial" (by 25-30% in most tests).
- Use action verbs: "Start," "Get," "Try," "Unlock," "Join."
- Include the value: "Get 4 Free Agents" > "Sign Up."
- Remove risk: "No credit card required" beneath the button.

### CTA Color and Placement
- The CTA should be the most visually prominent element on the page.
- High contrast with background. If the page is dark, the button is bright. Vice versa.
- Above the fold on landing pages. At the end of each section on long pages.
- Repeat the CTA 3-5 times on a long landing page (with slightly different copy each time).

## 7. Headline Formulas

### Formula 1: Number + Adjective + Noun + Promise
- "44 Specialized AI Agents to Handle Any Task"
- "4 Free AI Experts Ready to Work for You"
- "One Powerful Subscription That Replaces 5 AI Tools"

### Formula 2: How to [Desired Outcome] Without [Pain Point]
- "How to Get Expert AI Help Without Paying Enterprise Prices"
- "How to Write Better Marketing Copy Without Hiring a Copywriter"
- "How to Debug Code Without Spending Hours on Stack Overflow"

### Formula 3: [Question That Resonates]
- "What If You Had an Expert for Every Question?"
- "Tired of Generic AI Answers?"
- "What Would You Do with 44 AI Specialists on Your Team?"

### Formula 4: [Direct Command]
- "Stop Settling for Generic AI."
- "Upgrade Your AI. Upgrade Your Output."
- "Meet Your New Team of 44 AI Experts."

### Formula 5: [Contrast/Comparison]
- "One Generalist vs 44 Specialists. Which Would You Choose?"
- "The AI Tool for People Who Outgrew ChatGPT."
- "Beyond Chat. Beyond Generic. Beyond One-Size-Fits-All."

### Formula 6: [Result + Timeframe]
- "Better Answers in 30 Seconds — From the Right Specialist."
- "From Question to Expert Answer in Under a Minute."
- "Your First AI Specialist Conversation Starts Now."

### Headline Testing
- Always A/B test headlines. The "best" headline is the one that converts, not the one that sounds best.
- Test radically different approaches first (question vs command vs benefit). Then optimize the winning approach.
- Headlines account for 50-80% of landing page performance. Invest time here.

## 8. Tier Comparison Copy

### How to Describe Each Tier

**FREE Tier**
- Position: The trial. The taste test. The no-risk entry point.
- Copy: "Start with 4 AI specialists — free forever. No credit card. No strings."
- Emphasis: Zero risk. Real value. Not a crippled demo.
- Avoid: Making it sound like a lesser product. FREE should feel generous, not stingy.

**STARTER ($19.99/month)**
- Position: The commitment. "I'm serious about AI."
- Copy: "16 specialist agents for less than a dollar each. The essentials for getting real work done."
- Emphasis: Value per agent. Broad capability. The best first paid step.
- Avoid: Comparing unfavorably to PLUS. STARTER should feel complete for its tier.

**PLUS ($49.99/month)**
- Position: The sweet spot. Most value per dollar.
- Copy: "30 AI specialists — nearly every expert you need. The plan most users choose."
- Emphasis: "Most popular." Breadth of coverage. Social proof.
- Badge: "MOST POPULAR" or "BEST VALUE."
- Avoid: Making it sound like a compromise between STARTER and SMART.

**SMART ($99.99/month, $84.99 annual)**
- Position: The premium. Cloud AI powered. For users who demand the best output.
- Copy: "39 agents powered by Claude Sonnet — the premium AI model. When good enough isn't good enough."
- Emphasis: Quality difference. Cloud AI. Intelligence upgrade. For professionals.
- Avoid: Making it seem like "just more agents." The model upgrade is the story.

**PRO ($200/month, $170 annual)**
- Position: The complete package. Everything. No limits.
- Copy: "Every agent. Every feature. No ceiling. For users who want it all."
- Emphasis: Completeness. Priority access. Premium status.
- Avoid: Per-agent value comparison (it's poor). PRO sells on completeness, not per-unit economics.

### Tier Comparison Table Copy
When displaying all tiers in a table:
- Use clear feature names, not jargon.
- Checkmarks for included features, dashes for excluded.
- Highlight the differences between adjacent tiers.
- Add a "Recommended for" line: "STARTER: Individual users. PLUS: Power users. SMART: Professionals. PRO: Teams and businesses."

### Upgrade Copy Between Tiers
- STARTER → PLUS: "14 more specialists for $30/month. That's $2.14 per additional expert."
- PLUS → SMART: "Upgrade your AI brain. Claude Sonnet delivers noticeably smarter, more nuanced answers."
- SMART → PRO: "Get the complete arsenal. Every agent, no exceptions. For $100 more, close every gap."

## 10. Voice and Tone Guide: Stone AI Brand Voice

### Core Voice Attributes

**Confident, Not Arrogant**
- We know our product is good. We don't beg for attention.
- YES: "44 specialist agents. One subscription."
- NO: "We think our agents might be the best, but you decide!"

**Accessible, Not Dumbed Down**
- We explain things clearly. We don't condescend.
- YES: "Each agent is trained for a specific job — like having a team of experts."
- NO: "Our super-smart AI agents use advanced neural networks to process your queries!"
- Also NO: "Our little helpers try their best to answer your questions!"

**Practical, Not Hype**
- We talk about what the product does, not what AI could theoretically do someday.
- YES: "The Code Agent debugs Python, JavaScript, and TypeScript."
- NO: "Our revolutionary AI will transform the way humanity interacts with technology!"

**Direct, Not Abrasive**
- We get to the point. We don't pad.
- YES: "Start free. Upgrade when you're ready."
- NO: "We cordially invite you to explore our complimentary tier at your earliest convenience."

**Human, Not Corporate**
- We sound like a person, not a press release.
- YES: "We built Stone AI because we were tired of generic AI answers."
- NO: "Stone AI leverages cutting-edge artificial intelligence to deliver synergistic solutions."

### Tone Adjustments by Context

**Landing Page**: Confident, energetic, direct. Short sentences. Bold claims (backed by evidence).
**Pricing Page**: Clear, transparent, helpful. No confusion, no hidden information.
**Upgrade Prompts**: Helpful, not pushy. "You might want this" not "BUY NOW."
**Error Messages**: Apologetic, clear, actionable. "Something went wrong. Here's what to do."
**Emails**: Warm, personal, brief. Write like you're emailing a smart friend.
**Support**: Patient, thorough, empathetic. Solve the problem completely.
**Forum**: Casual, community-oriented, encouraging.

### Words We Use
- Specialists, experts, agents (not "bots" or "chatbots")
- Upgrade (not "buy" or "purchase")
- Plans (not "packages" or "bundles")
- Start free (not "free trial" — ours is free forever, not time-limited)
- Powered by (not "built on" or "leveraging")

### Words We Avoid
- Revolutionary, game-changing, disruptive (hype words)
- Leverage, synergy, paradigm (corporate jargon)
- AI-powered (overused, meaningless)
- Best in class, world-class (unsubstantiated superlatives)
- Simply, just, easy (minimizing words that can frustrate users if something isn't easy)

### Stone AI's One-Line Identity
"44 AI specialists. One subscription. Every expert you need."

This line works because:
- Specific number (44) is credible and memorable.
- "Specialists" is the key differentiator from generalist AI.
- "One subscription" addresses multi-tool fatigue.
- "Every expert you need" is the promise.

## 11. Landing Page Copy Structure

### Section-by-Section Blueprint

**Hero Section**
- Headline: Use formula from Section 7. Test multiple.
- Subheadline: One sentence expanding on the headline.
- CTA: Primary action button.
- Social proof: User count or trust indicator.
- Visual: Screenshot or illustration showing the chat interface with multiple agents.

**Problem Section**
- Header: "The Problem with Generic AI"
- 3 pain points with brief descriptions:
  1. Generic answers that need heavy editing.
  2. Juggling multiple AI tools.
  3. No specialization, no depth.

**Solution Section**
- Header: "Meet Your AI Specialist Team"
- Showcase 4-6 agents with icons, names, and one-line descriptions.
- CTA: "Explore all 40 agents"

**Social Proof Section**
- 3 testimonials following BAB format.
- User count / activity metric.
- Trust badges.

**Pricing Section**
- Tier comparison table (simplified — 3 tiers visible, expand for all 5).
- "Most Popular" badge on target tier.
- CTA for each tier.

**FAQ Section**
- 5-7 common questions.
- Address objections: price, comparison to ChatGPT, data security, cancellation.

**Final CTA Section**
- Restate the headline (different wording).
- Single strong CTA.
- Risk removal: "Free forever. No credit card. Cancel anytime."

## 12. Conversion Copy Metrics

### What to Measure

**Landing Page**
- Bounce rate (target: <60%).
- Time on page (target: 60+ seconds).
- Scroll depth (target: 50%+ reach pricing section).
- CTA click rate (target: 3-8%).
- Signup conversion (target: 2-5%).

**Email**
- Open rate (target: 25-40% for transactional, 15-25% for marketing).
- Click rate (target: 3-8%).
- Unsubscribe rate (target: <0.5% per email).

**In-App Prompts**
- Impression-to-click rate (target: 5-15%).
- Upgrade conversion from prompt (target: 1-5%).

### Copy A/B Testing Rules
1. Test one element at a time (headline OR CTA OR image, not all three).
2. Run tests for statistical significance (usually 1,000+ impressions per variant).
3. Test big changes first (entirely different headlines), then optimize the winner.
4. The control stays until a variant beats it at 95% confidence.
5. Document every test and result. Build a library of winning copy patterns.
