# PR & Media Relations — Stone AI Ecosystem

## Executive Overview

Public relations and media coverage provide something that no amount of paid advertising can buy: third-party credibility. When TechCrunch, The Verge, or a respected AI publication writes about Stone AI, they are implicitly endorsing the product with their editorial reputation. This earned media builds trust at scale, drives high-quality traffic, creates permanent backlinks for SEO, and positions the founder and company as authorities in the AI space.

PR is not advertising. You cannot buy editorial coverage (ethically). You earn it by being genuinely newsworthy — having a compelling story, building something notable, achieving meaningful milestones, or providing expert insight when journalists need sources. This seed covers how to build a PR engine that generates consistent media coverage for the Three-Headed Monster ecosystem.

## Media List Building

### Target Publication Categories

**Tier 1: Major Tech Publications (Dream targets)**
- TechCrunch, The Verge, Wired, Ars Technica, Engadget
- Business: Forbes Tech, Bloomberg Technology, Business Insider Tech
- AI-specific: VentureBeat AI, MIT Technology Review, AI News
- Reach: Millions of monthly readers, massive authority

**Tier 2: SaaS and Startup Publications (High value)**
- SaaStr, SaaS Mag, Product Hunt (for launches), Hacker News (community)
- Startup-focused: TechStartups, Startup Daily, Startup Grind
- Reach: Hundreds of thousands, highly targeted audience

**Tier 3: Industry Vertical Publications (Niche but high-converting)**
- Productivity: Lifehacker, MakeUseOf, How-To Geek
- Developer: Dev.to, Hacker Noon, CSS-Tricks, Smashing Magazine
- Mobile: 9to5Mac, Android Authority, Phone Arena (for Best AI Mobile)
- Reach: Tens to hundreds of thousands, specific audiences

**Tier 4: Podcasts and YouTube (Growing authority)**
- AI podcasts: The AI Podcast, Practical AI, Lex Fridman (aspirational)
- Tech YouTube: MKBHD (aspirational), Fireship, Traversy Media
- SaaS podcasts: SaaS Revolution Show, The SaaS Podcast
- Reach: Varies, but highly engaged audiences

**Tier 5: Newsletters and Blogs (Consistent coverage)**
- The Neuron, TLDR, Morning Brew Tech, Ben's Bites
- Individual bloggers and AI thought leaders
- Reach: Thousands to hundreds of thousands, high open rates

### Building Your Media Database

For each journalist/editor, track:

```
Name: [Full name]
Publication: [Name]
Beat: [AI, SaaS, startups, mobile, developer tools]
Email: [verified email]
Twitter/X: [@handle]
LinkedIn: [URL]
Past Articles: [Links to 3-5 relevant pieces they've written]
Relationship Status: [Cold / Warm / Active / Past coverage]
Last Contact: [Date]
Notes: [What they're interested in, past interactions]
```

**Finding Journalist Contacts:**
- Read bylines on relevant articles (the journalist's name is right there)
- Check Twitter bios (many journalists list their beat and contact)
- LinkedIn (search by publication name + "reporter" or "editor")
- Press pages of similar companies (see who covers competitors)
- HARO, Qwoted, Source of Sources (journalists post queries, you respond)
- Muck Rack, Cision (paid PR databases — worth it when scaling)

### Relationship Building (Before You Need Coverage)

The biggest mistake in PR is only contacting journalists when you want something. Build relationships first:

1. **Follow their work**: Read and engage with their articles genuinely
2. **Share their articles**: Retweet, quote tweet with thoughtful commentary
3. **Be a source**: Respond to their Twitter questions, HARO queries — be helpful without pitching
4. **Offer expertise**: "If you ever need a source on AI agent technology, I'm happy to help"
5. **Meet in person**: If they attend conferences, say hello. Real relationships beat cold pitches.
6. **Respect their time**: Journalists are overwhelmed with pitches. Be brief, be relevant, be human.

## Newsworthy Story Angles

### Types of Stories Journalists Want

**1. Trend Stories**: "Here's where the AI industry is heading"
- Stone AI angle: The shift from general-purpose to specialized AI agents
- Data needed: Market trends, user behavior data, expert perspective

**2. Founder Stories**: "How this founder is building something different"
- Stone AI angle: Three businesses, one ecosystem, bootstrapped approach
- Pitch to: Startup-focused publications, founder interview podcasts

**3. Product Stories**: "This new tool does something interesting"
- Stone AI angle: 40 agents vs. one chatbot, Bestie personalization
- Best for: Product launches, major feature releases, milestone announcements

**4. Contrarian Stories**: "Why the conventional wisdom is wrong"
- Stone AI angle: "The future of AI isn't one model that does everything — it's specialists"
- Best for: Op-eds, thought leadership, Twitter threads that get picked up

**5. Data Stories**: "The numbers reveal something surprising"
- Stone AI angle: User behavior data (which agents are most used, how Bestie changes engagement, conversion patterns)
- Best for: Research-oriented publications, data journalism

**6. Human Interest Stories**: "How real people use this"
- Stone AI angle: User stories of transformation (saved time, improved output, discovered capabilities)
- Best for: Lifestyle and productivity publications

## Crisis PR

### Crisis Categories

**Level 1: Minor (Internal resolution)**
- Single negative review, minor bug, user complaint on social media
- Response: Direct customer support, fix issue, no public statement needed

**Level 2: Moderate (Proactive communication)**
- Service outage, data delay (not breach), pricing controversy, feature removal backlash
- Response: Status page update, social media acknowledgment, email to affected users

**Level 3: Severe (Full crisis response)**
- Data breach, security vulnerability, major outage (hours), legal action, viral negative coverage
- Response: Full crisis protocol (see below)

### Crisis Response Protocol

```
HOUR 0-1: ASSESS
├── What happened? (factual, not speculative)
├── Who is affected? (scope of impact)
├── Is it ongoing or resolved?
├── Who knows? (internal and external)
└── What's the worst-case scenario?

HOUR 1-4: CONTAIN
├── Fix the issue (engineering priority #1)
├── Draft holding statement (acknowledge, don't speculate)
├── Notify Clerk/Stripe/partners if affected
├── Brief team on messaging (one voice, consistent story)
└── Set up monitoring (social, email, forum, press)

HOUR 4-24: COMMUNICATE
├── Publish official statement (blog, email, social)
├── Respond to press inquiries (prepared statement)
├── Update status page
├── Direct customer communication (email to affected users)
└── Monitor and correct misinformation

DAY 2-7: RESOLVE
├── Root cause analysis
├── Publish post-mortem (transparent, technical, honest)
├── Implement fixes to prevent recurrence
├── Follow up with affected users (apology + remediation)
└── Update security/privacy documentation if needed

DAY 7+: RECOVER
├── Continue monitoring sentiment
├── Proactive positive coverage (new feature, milestone)
├── Evaluate and improve crisis playbook
└── Train team on lessons learned
```

### Crisis Statement Template

```
[HEADLINE: Acknowledge the situation]

On [date], we became aware of [brief description of what happened].

[WHAT WE KNOW]
[Factual description of the incident — no speculation, no blame]

```

## DIY PR Checklist (Before Hiring an Agency)

Before spending $5,000-$20,000/month on a PR agency, exhaust these DIY approaches:

```
□ Build media list of 50+ relevant journalists
□ Set up Google Alerts for brand name + competitor names
□ Create HARO account and respond to 5+ queries per week
□ Write and distribute 1 press release per month
□ Pitch 10 journalists per month (personalized, not mass email)
□ Publish 1 thought leadership article per month (LinkedIn, Medium)
□ Engage journalists on Twitter daily (genuine, not transactional)
□ Create a press page on stone-ai.net (logos, fact sheet, founder bio)
□ Prepare a media kit (company overview, key stats, product screenshots)
□ Track all coverage and measure impact

When to hire a PR agency:
- You've maxed out DIY capacity and need to scale
- You're preparing for a major launch (Best AI Mobile, Stone AI Tools)
- You need crisis PR support
- You have budget for $5K+/month and ROAS expectations are clear
```
