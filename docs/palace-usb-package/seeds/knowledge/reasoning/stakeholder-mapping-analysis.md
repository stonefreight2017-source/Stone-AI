## Purpose

Every strategic decision affects and is affected by stakeholders — people and organizations with influence over or interest in the outcome. Mismanaging stakeholders can kill a technically sound strategy. Cardinal maps stakeholder landscapes to help the founder navigate the human dimension of strategic decisions.

---

## 1. Power/Interest Grids

### The Framework

Plot stakeholders on two dimensions:
- **Power**: Ability to influence outcomes (high or low)
- **Interest**: Degree of concern about the outcome (high or low)

```
                     High Power
                         |
    "Keep Satisfied"     |      "Manage Closely"
    (High power,         |      (High power,
     Low interest)       |       High interest)
                         |
  Low ───────────────────+──────────────── High
  Interest               |              Interest
                         |
    "Monitor"            |      "Keep Informed"
    (Low power,          |      (Low power,
     Low interest)       |       High interest)
                         |
                     Low Power
```

### Stakeholder Engagement Strategy by Quadrant

**Manage Closely** (High Power, High Interest):
- These stakeholders can make or break your initiative
- Engage frequently, involve in decisions, manage expectations
- Examples: Key investors, major customers, regulatory authorities

**Keep Satisfied** (High Power, Low Interest):
- Powerful but currently not focused on your area
- Keep them informed enough that they don't become blockers
- Don't overwhelm them with details they don't care about
- Examples: Platform providers (Vercel, Stripe), industry associations

**Keep Informed** (Low Power, High Interest):
- Interested and engaged but limited direct influence
- Provide regular updates, gather their input
- Can become advocates or critics depending on how you treat them
- Examples: Community members, users on free tier, bloggers

**Monitor** (Low Power, Low Interest):
- Minimal engagement needed
- Check periodically for changes in power or interest
- Examples: Peripheral industry observers, distant competitors

## 2. Influence Networks

### Mapping Influence Beyond Formal Power

Formal power (authority, money, legal) is only part of the picture. Informal influence often matters more:

**Types of Influence**:
- **Positional**: Authority from role or title (CEO, regulator, investor)
- **Expertise**: Influence from knowledge or skills (thought leaders, advisors)
- **Relational**: Influence from connections (networkers, community leaders)
- **Informational**: Control over information flow (media, analysts)
- **Reputational**: Influence from credibility and track record (industry veterans)

### Network Mapping Process

1. **List all stakeholders** (people and organizations)
2. **Map connections**: Who knows who? Who influences who?
3. **Identify brokers**: Who connects otherwise disconnected groups?
4. **Identify gatekeepers**: Who controls access to key resources or people?
5. **Identify amplifiers**: Who can spread messages to large audiences?

### Key Influence Positions

**Connectors**: People who bridge different groups
- Example: A developer who's active in both the AI community and the gaming community
- Value: Can introduce your product to new audiences

**Maven**: People who accumulate and share knowledge
- Example: Tech reviewers, newsletter authors, "that person everyone asks for recommendations"
- Value: Their endorsement carries disproportionate weight

**Salespeople**: People who naturally persuade others
- Example: Enthusiastic users who naturally evangelize
- Value: Organic growth engine (word of mouth)

Cardinal tracks these individuals specifically because a relationship with ONE connector can be worth more than a $10K ad campaign.

## 3. Coalition Building

### When You Need Coalitions

Coalitions are necessary when:
- You can't achieve the goal alone (need allies)
- Multiple stakeholders must agree for something to happen
- You need to counter an opposing force
- A collective voice carries more weight than an individual one

### Coalition Building Process

**Step 1: Define the Objective**
What specifically do you want the coalition to achieve?

**Step 2: Identify Potential Allies**
Who shares your interest (even if for different reasons)?
- Common interest: They want the same outcome you want
- Common enemy: They oppose the same thing you oppose
- Complementary assets: They have what you need, you have what they need

**Step 3: Assess Coalition Fit**

| Potential Ally | Shared Interest | Their Influence | Reliability | Cost of Alliance |
|---------------|-----------------|-----------------|-------------|------------------|
| Ally A | High | High | Medium | Low |
| Ally B | Medium | High | High | Medium |
| Ally C | High | Low | High | Low |

**Step 4: Build the Coalition**
- Start with the most aligned and reliable allies
- Be explicit about shared goals and mutual benefits
- Define roles and contributions
- Establish communication channels
- Set expectations about commitment level

**Step 5: Maintain the Coalition**
- Regular communication
- Celebrate shared wins
- Address tensions early
- Be fair in distributing benefits and credit

### Coalition Types for Stone AI

**User coalition**: Power users who advocate for Stone AI
- Build: Featured user program, beta access, community leadership roles
- Benefit: Organic marketing, product feedback, social proof

**Developer/creator coalition**: People who build on or with Stone AI
- Build: API access, developer documentation, community support
- Benefit: Ecosystem growth, integration network effects

**Industry coalition**: Other small AI companies facing similar regulatory/market challenges
- Build: Industry association participation, shared advocacy
- Benefit: Collective voice on regulation, shared best practices

**Platform coalition**: Strategic alignment with infrastructure providers
- Build: Case studies, featured partnerships, technical integration
- Benefit: Visibility, co-marketing, potential technical advantages

## 4. Stakeholder Communication Strategies

### Tailoring Communication by Stakeholder Type

**Investors / Board** (results-oriented):
- Lead with metrics and outcomes
- Focus on growth trajectory and milestones
- Address risks proactively with mitigation plans
- Format: Concise deck or dashboard, quarterly updates

**Customers** (value-oriented):
- Lead with benefits to them
- Focus on new features, improvements, roadmap
- Address issues with transparency and resolution timeline
- Format: Email updates, in-app announcements, changelog

**Team Members** (mission-oriented):
- Lead with the "why" behind decisions
- Focus on impact of their work and company direction
- Address concerns with honesty and context
- Format: All-hands, team meetings, written memos

**Press / Analysts** (story-oriented):
- Lead with a compelling narrative
- Focus on what's new, different, or noteworthy
- Provide data and quotes they can use
- Format: Press releases, briefings, interviews

**Regulators** (compliance-oriented):
- Lead with your compliance efforts
- Focus on how you protect users and society
- Be proactive about transparency
- Format: Formal communications, compliance reports

**Community** (belonging-oriented):
- Lead with shared values and community wins
- Focus on what the community has built together
- Celebrate contributors and milestones
- Format: Forum posts, social media, community events

### Communication Frequency Matrix

| Stakeholder | Normal Times | During Changes | During Crisis |
|-------------|-------------|----------------|---------------|
| Key investors | Monthly | Weekly | Daily |
| Paying users | Bi-weekly | Weekly | As needed |
| Free users | Monthly | Bi-weekly | As needed |
| Team | Daily | Daily (more context) | Continuous |
| Press | Quarterly | As needed | Rapid response |
| Regulators | As required | Proactive | Immediate |
| Community | Weekly | Daily | Continuous |

### Message Framing by Audience

The same decision can be framed differently for different audiences:

**Decision: Raising SMART tier from $99.99 to $109.99**

- To investors: "We're adjusting pricing to improve unit economics and better reflect the value delivered. Expected impact: +8% ARPU with <2% churn increase."
- To users: "We're investing in major improvements to the SMART tier, including [new features]. To support this, we're adjusting pricing for new subscribers starting [date]. Existing subscribers are locked in at the current rate for 12 months."
- To team: "Our SMART tier pricing experiment showed strong willingness to pay. We're adjusting up to fund the features on the Q3 roadmap."
- To community: "Big improvements coming to SMART tier! Here's what we're building next: [roadmap]. We're adjusting pricing for new subscribers to fund these improvements."

Same decision. Different frame. Different emphasis. All truthful.

## 5. Stakeholder Risk Analysis

### Stakeholder-Driven Risks

Each stakeholder can create risks:

| Stakeholder | Risk | Probability | Impact | Mitigation |
|-------------|------|------------|--------|------------|
| Paying users | Mass churn from price change | Medium | High | Grandfather existing users |
| Vercel | Platform changes or pricing increase | Low | High | Maintain ability to migrate |
| Regulators | New compliance requirements | Medium | Medium | Build compliance readiness |
| Competitors | Feature copy or price war | High | Medium | Differentiate on non-copyable dimensions |
| Press | Negative coverage | Low | Medium | Proactive PR, rapid response plan |
| Key employees | Departure | Medium | High | Knowledge documentation, competitive comp |

### Stakeholder Dependency Assessment

How dependent is Stone AI on each stakeholder?

| Stakeholder | Dependency Level | Can We Replace? | Switch Cost | Risk Rating |
|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|-------------|-------------|
| Vercel | High | Yes (other hosts) | Medium | Medium |
| Neon DB | High | Yes (other PG hosts) | Medium | Medium |
| Clerk | High | Yes (Auth.js, etc) | High | Medium-High |
| Stripe | High | Yes (Lemonsqueezy, etc) | Medium | Medium |
| Anthropic API | Medium | Yes (other providers) | Low (abstracted) | Low |
| Cloudflare | Medium | Yes (other CDN/DNS) | Low | Low |

**Cardinal's principle**: No single stakeholder dependency should be existential. Maintain the ability to migrate away from every dependency within 30 days.

## 6. Stakeholder Engagement Playbooks

### Playbook: Launching a Major Feature

```
PRE-LAUNCH (2 weeks before):
□ Brief key investors on the feature and expected impact
□ Share preview with top 20 paying users for early feedback
□ Prepare press materials (if newsworthy)
□ Alert community leaders / moderators
□ Verify all vendor/platform dependencies are stable

LAUNCH DAY:
□ Email announcement to all users (segment messaging by tier)
□ In-app announcement with feature tour
□ Social media posts (platform-specific)
□ Forum announcement with discussion thread
□ Press outreach (if applicable)
□ Monitor: sentiment, bugs, support volume

POST-LAUNCH (1 week after):
□ Share adoption metrics with investors
□ Thank early testers and community contributors
□ Respond to all feedback (positive and negative)
□ Address any bugs or issues publicly
□ Retrospective on launch process
```

### Playbook: Handling a Service Disruption

```
IMMEDIATE (0-30 minutes):
□ Status page update: "Investigating"
□ Internal assessment: scope, cause, ETA
□ In-app banner if users are affected

SHORT-TERM (30 min - 2 hours):
□ Status page update with details
□ Email to affected paying users
□ Social media acknowledgment
□ Community forum post
□ Investor alert if significant

RESOLUTION (when fixed):
□ Status page: "Resolved" with summary
□ Email to affected users: what happened, what we did, what we'll do to prevent
□ Community post-mortem (appropriate detail level)
□ Internal post-mortem (full detail)

FOLLOW-UP (1 week after):
□ Implement prevention measures
□ Update users on prevention measures
□ Update investors in next regular update
```

## 7. Stakeholder Intelligence

### Gathering Stakeholder Intelligence

Understanding stakeholder motivations, concerns, and influence requires active intelligence gathering:

**For investors/partners**:
- What are their current priorities? (portfolio focus, fund thesis)
- What metrics do they care about most?
- What are their concerns about your market?
- Who else in their network could be helpful?

**For customers**:
- What job are they hiring your product for?
- What alternatives are they considering?
- What would make them leave? What would make them upgrade?
- Who makes the buying decision? Who influences it?

**For regulators**:
- What enforcement actions have they taken recently?
- What guidance have they published?
- What's on their agenda for the next 6-12 months?
- Who are the key individuals driving policy?

**For competitors**:
- Who are their stakeholders? (investors, partners, key customers)
- Where are their stakeholder relationships weak?
- Are any of their stakeholders persuadable?
- What coalitions are they building?

## Summary

Stakeholder mapping ensures the founder navigates the human dimension of strategy with the same rigor as the technical and financial dimensions. Cardinal's approach:

1. **Map**: Identify all stakeholders and plot on Power/Interest grid
2. **Analyze**: Understand influence networks, dependencies, and motivations
3. **Strategize**: Tailor engagement approach by stakeholder quadrant
4. **Build coalitions**: Identify and cultivate strategic alliances
5. **Communicate**: Frame messages appropriately for each audience
6. **Monitor**: Track stakeholder risks and dependency exposure
7. **Maintain playbooks**: Ready-made responses for common stakeholder scenarios

No strategy succeeds without stakeholder alignment. Cardinal ensures the founder sees the full landscape of human dynamics surrounding every strategic decision.
